ADAPT · ENGAGE · HOLD

A small clamp that finds its own fit.

A spring lets the toothed runner adjust to the paper stack. As the arm closes, a separate engager meets the fixed frame and slides underneath the pawl. Its raised surface is intended to seat and support the pawl in the teeth.

Explore the proposed arrangement below. Motion is illustrative; a working lock and repeatable grip force have not yet been established.

THE PROPOSAL

Three jobs, three separate pieces.

  1. Find the stack. The blue B runner slides inside the A arm. An internal spring supplies compliance during adaptation.
  2. Trigger engagement. The gold engager stays parallel to A. Its rear end contacts a fixed stop; further closing produces relative sliding motion.
  3. Support the pawl. The purple, two-arm pawl pivots on A. The engager’s continuous raised surface is intended to keep it seated. A separate return spring restores the engager on opening.
WHAT WE KNOW

A concept with visible test boundaries.

Checked: an ideal rear-stop travel calculation across A = 100–180° gives 4 mm of engager stroke, with the contact point staying on the proposed stop.

Still to solve: full solid clearance, tooth seating, retained lock stability, release timing, friction, spring specifications and actual clamp force. The tooth capture and pawl motion shown here are prescribed.

Paper thickness changes the illustrative adaptation. It does not represent a verified capacity range. Clear board and cutaway plates are inspection aids.
