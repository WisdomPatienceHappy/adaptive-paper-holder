ADAPT · ENGAGE · HOLD

A small clamp that finds its own fit.

A spring lets the toothed runner adjust to the paper stack. As the arm closes, a separate engager meets the fixed frame and slides underneath the pawl. Its raised surface is intended to seat and support the pawl in the teeth.

THE PROPOSAL

Three jobs, three separate pieces.

  1. Find the stack. The blue B runner slides inside the A arm. An internal spring supplies compliance during adaptation.
  2. Trigger engagement. The gold engager stays parallel to A. Its rear end contacts a fixed stop; further closing produces relative sliding motion.
  3. Support the pawl. The purple, two-arm pawl pivots on A. The engager’s continuous raised surface is intended to keep it seated. A separate return spring restores the engager on opening.
WHAT WE KNOW

A concept with visible test boundaries.

Checked: an ideal rear-stop travel calculation across A = 100–180° gives 4 mm of engager stroke, with the contact point staying on the proposed stop.

Still to solve: full solid clearance, tooth seating, retained lock stability, release timing, friction, spring specifications and actual clamp force. A/B motion and tooth capture are prescribed. The optional tooth-clearance check stops the whole pawl at first interference; it does not solve the engager contact. All four tested closing paths currently block before full seating.

Paper thickness changes the illustrative adaptation. It does not represent a verified capacity range. Clear board and cutaway plates are inspection aids.

Equations, assumptions and reproduction

Pad force: Q = Kpad · max(0, paper + padFree − gap). With outward runner coordinate r, the assumed latch must react R = −FB − Q·∂gap/∂r. The closed stop can hold only when net closing torque Q·∂gap/∂θ − FE·∂stroke/∂θ ≥ 0, under this reduced model.

Assumed B spring: 0.6 N preload + 0.05 N/mm × (33 mm − r). Assumed engager spring: 0.02 N preload + 0.02 N/mm × stroke. They are sensitivity inputs, not specifications of the pictured coils. Friction, actual tooth and cam reactions, structural compliance and stability of the pawl remain unsolved. A 40 mm hand moment arm is used only for initial threshold estimates in the receipt, not a complete operating effort curve.
